MESSPa — Embodiment Stimulation Shoulder Pad

Image coming soon

MESSPa (Multi-modal Embodiment Stimulation Shoulder Pad) addresses a specific problem with detachable supernumerary robotic arms: an arm worn on the body gives a strong sense of agency, but once detached and operated remotely — so the user can act in two places at once — that sense of agency, and task performance, both tend to drop.

The shoulder pad combines two things: one-to-one motion transfer from an HTC VIVE tracker on the wrist, so the operator drives the detached arm with their own body, and positional haptic feedback from four pneumatic balloons on the shoulder (one per task quadrant, 80 mm spacing, 0.05 MPa / 13 N max force), which signal where the remote arm currently is.

We tested it in a 2×2 within-subject study (N = 12) crossing feedback (with/without MESSPa) and attachment (on-shoulder vs. detached), under single- and dual-task load. With the haptic feedback, detached-condition task completion time became statistically indistinguishable from the on-shoulder condition, and the sense of agency improved significantly (p = 0.004). Sense of ownership did not change.

Result: Adding positional shoulder haptics to a detached, motion-transferred arm closed the performance gap to wearing the arm on the body, and measurably restored the operator's sense of agency — without changing the sense of ownership.
