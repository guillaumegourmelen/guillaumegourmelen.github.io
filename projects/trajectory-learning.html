This page covers my PhD thesis as a whole — Embodied Robot Systems: Control and Learning for Skill-Transfer from Humans (LIRMM-CNRS / Université de Montpellier, 2022, NNT: 2022UMONS091), conducted within the JST ERATO Jizai Body project. The thesis is centred on one core question: how can a robot reproduce not just a human's trajectory, but their physical interaction quality — the stiffness and damping they apply, and how these change moment to moment during a task.

The main experimental contribution — online estimation of human arm impedance from a haptic device alone, and its use to drive a robot's admittance controller during assistive teleoperation — was published at IEEE IROS 2021 and is detailed on the 7DoF Teleoperation & Impedance Adaptation page. The thesis manuscript situates that result within a broader review of human motor control, impedance estimation methods, and teleoperation architectures, and discusses extensions and follow-up directions explored during the PhD.

Note: This page and the 7DoF Teleoperation page describe the same underlying study (IEEE IROS 2021) from two angles — this one as part of the full thesis, the other as the standalone conference paper. There is no second, separate experiment.
