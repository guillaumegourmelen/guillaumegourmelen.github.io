Reconfigurable AUV for Karst Exploration

Image coming soon

This project, developed at LIRMM (Université de Montpellier), is a dynamically reconfigurable Autonomous Underwater Vehicle nicknamed the "Umbrella Robot" — built for karst cave exploration, where narrow, irregular, flooded conduits are inaccessible to conventional ROVs. It carries seven thrusters split across a forward section (three thrusters) and a rear section (four thrusters).

The "umbrella" name comes directly from how it reconfigures: at the centre of the robot sits an endless-screw (worm-gear) mechanism, the same kind of mechanism that opens and closes a real umbrella's canopy. Turning this single screw drives all the thruster arms to swing open or fold inward together, exactly like umbrella ribs — so the robot can fold into a compact, torpedo-like shape to cross a narrow passage, or open fully for six-DoF station-keeping and inspection, using only two motors to set the angle of all seven thrusters, rather than one motor per thruster.

My contribution was the complete physical build: mechanical design, CNC-machined and 3D-printed parts, the waterproof housing, and the umbrella/endless-screw reconfiguration mechanism itself, plus the electronics to drive it. The robot I built was subsequently used by Tho Dang — my co-author on the resulting paper — throughout his PhD thesis at LIRMM, where he extended the control allocation and energy-efficiency work built on this same physical platform.

Why reconfigurable? A fixed-configuration AUV has to trade off speed against maneuverability. Thanks to the umbrella mechanism, this robot's thrusters can swing from a torpedo-like layout (fast, narrow-passage transit) to a fully open layout (6-DoF control during station-keeping or inspection), all driven by the same two motors.
