Overview

Body Sublimation

Image coming soon

Body Sublimation is an interaction approach that lets a user physically manipulate distant objects through a hidden robotic arm, while only ever seeing a single virtual hand — their own original hand and the robot are both visually removed via diminished reality. The goal is to reconcile two things that normally trade off against each other: the perceptual flexibility of XR (fast, unconstrained, embodied) and the physical capability of a real robot (slow, constrained, but able to actually touch the world).

The virtual hand operates in two states, named after material phase transitions: a solid state, when the hidden robotic hand matches the virtual hand's position and can grasp or manipulate real objects, and a gas state, when it doesn't — letting the virtual hand pass through obstacles, extend past the robot's workspace, or move freely while the robot autonomously handles collision avoidance out of view. The transition between states becomes the only signal the user needs about what the system can currently do, without ever showing them the robot itself.

The prototype uses a Meta Quest 3 (hand tracking, Gaussian-splat–based diminished reality, virtual hand rendering) paired with an xArm7 fitted with a PSYONIC Ability Hand as the physical end effector. A 12-participant within-subject study compared this approach against a pass-through baseline (robot and real hand both visible) across reaching, reaching-with-obstacle, and grasping tasks.

Result: Removing the robot from view — and replacing it with a single coherent virtual hand whose state communicates the robot's constraints — significantly improved sense of ownership, agency, and trajectory straightness around obstacles, without hurting task performance.
