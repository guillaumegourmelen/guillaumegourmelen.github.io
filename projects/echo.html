Overview

Echocardiography Teleoperation Robot

Image coming soon

Echocardiographic ultrasound examination requires a highly skilled sonographer to hold and maneuver a probe against the patient's chest with precise pressure and angulation. This is a skill-intensive, physically demanding task that is currently impossible to perform at full quality over distance.

This project, conducted at Waseda University's Iwata Lab, develops a haptic teleoperation system for cardiac ultrasound: a robot arm manipulates the ultrasound probe on the patient side, controlled by a remote sonographer via a haptic interface. The robot must reproduce not just position but the compliant, pressure-sensitive contact characteristic of expert probe manipulation.

My contribution focuses on Learning from Demonstration: recording expert sonographer sessions and training a model to extract the skill — the contact force profile, the probe orientation strategy, the scanning tempo — so that a less-experienced remote operator can be guided or augmented by the learned model in real time. This builds directly on the impedance-learning methodology developed during my PhD at LIRMM.

Motivation: Picture a robot performing a basic cardiac check at an everyday place — a gym, a city hall, a community health center — rather than at a hospital. Only the resulting data would be transmitted on to a hospital or cardiologist for review. That would let people learn their own health status without booking a hospital visit, and it would free practitioners from triaging routine, non-urgent checks one by one, so their time goes to the cases that actually need it.
