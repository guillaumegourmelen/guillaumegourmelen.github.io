MESSPa — Embodiment Stimulation Shoulder Pad

Project page: figures, results and BibTeX ↗

MESSPa (Multi-modal Embodiment Stimulation Shoulder Pad) addresses a specific problem with detachable supernumerary robotic arms: an arm worn on the body gives a strong sense of agency, but once detached and operated remotely — so the user can act in two places at once — that sense of agency, and task performance, both tend to drop.

The shoulder pad combines two things: one-to-one motion transfer from an HTC VIVE tracker on the shoulder pad (relative mapping, restricted to the 2-DoF horizontal plane), so the operator drives the detached arm (a MyCobot 280, 850 g, on a 3.5 kg harness) with their own body, and positional haptic feedback from four pneumatic balloons on the shoulder (25×25 mm, one per workspace quadrant, 80 mm spacing, 0.05 MPa supply / 13 N max force), which signal where the remote arm currently is.

It was tested in a 2×2 mixed-design study: interface (MESSPa vs. keyboard control) varied between groups — 13 volunteers used MESSPa, compared against 12 participants' keyboard-control data from a prior study (not randomly assigned) — crossed with attachment (on-shoulder vs. detached), varied within participants, under single- and dual-task load. Detaching the arm cost 0.6 s vs. 2.1 s more per target with MESSPa vs. keyboard in the single task, and 0.3 s vs. 1.8 s in the dual task. In the detached condition, MESSPa significantly improved the sense of agency (t = 2.582, df = 21, p = 0.017); the sense of ownership did not change (p = 0.734).

Result: Adding positional shoulder haptics to a detached, motion-transferred arm cut most of the performance cost of detaching it, and measurably restored the operator's sense of agency — without changing the sense of ownership.
