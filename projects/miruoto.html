Miruoto — Seeing Sound at the Stadium

Image coming soon

Miruoto (みるおと, "seeing sound") is a real-time system that renders the soundscape of a live sports match — ball impacts, footsteps, crowd reactions, applause — as onomatopoeia overlaid on large venue screens, in the style of sound effects in comics and manga. It targets a concrete gap: Deaf and hard-of-hearing spectators miss the auditory cues that carry much of a match's atmosphere and flow.

The system was built as a three-way collaboration led by design studio Hogaku Inc., with Aisin Corporation providing sound-recognition technology and Waseda University (Iwata Lab) contributing motion-based sound localization to determine where on the court a sound originates. The project was selected as a winning proposal at the Tokyo Metropolitan Government's UPGRADE with TOKYO pitch program, which led to Tokyo Metropolitan Government funding and support for bringing the system to the Deaflympics.

Miruoto was deployed on big screens at the Tokyo 2025 Deaflympics (15–26 November 2025) for the full duration of competition, from the early rounds through to the finals — table tennis at Tokyo Metropolitan Gymnasium and badminton at Keio Arena Tokyo — visualizing rally sounds and crowd noise live for every spectator in the venue. The system was also demonstrated earlier at ACM SIGGRAPH 2024 Emerging Technologies in Denver, and was used live on-air during TOKYO MX's televised Deaflympics broadcast.

Tokyo 2025 Deaflympics: Miruoto ran on venue screens throughout the table tennis and badminton competitions, from the opening matches through the finals, giving Deaf and hard-of-hearing spectators a real-time visual read of the game's sound and energy alongside hearing spectators.
